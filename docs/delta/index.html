The method, in plain terms

Delta computes Classic Delta (Burrows) distances with the R stylo package.

What the comparison runs

Stylometry observes recurring, measurable patterns in language use, such as how often common words recur. It does not read meaning, topics, or intent. Guided comparisons run 100, 300, 500, and 1,000 most-frequent-word settings together; 500 MFW opens first as a pre-specified display reference, not a best or optimal setting.

How to read distance

Nearer means more similar measured patterns inside this corpus and this setting. The numerical distance matrix is the primary evidence; the heatmap and the MDS projection are renderings of the same matrix, and the heatmap colour scale is local to the selected matrix. Axes of the MDS projection have no literary meaning; apparent clusters can be projection artefacts.

What stays visible

If a text's rights status is unknown, Delta records it. You can review the text, but it is not analysed and is not cleared for public redistribution. To run a comparison, each text needs documented permission to upload and analyse it. Delta keeps all four comparisons and every corpus warning side by side, with no winner-takes-all view and no confidence score. Do not conclude: nearer does not mean the same author, influence, intention, or authenticity.

Three ways to ask a stylometric question.

In this alpha, a limited part of the guided path has been tested end to end. The full workflows for all three research purposes have not yet been validated.

Every comparison is relative to your corpus. Choose the question first; Delta explains the choices that follow.

Not ready to upload? Open the workbench and choose "Explore the example", a recorded comparison of three short texts created for it.

Compare texts
Text against text

Which individual texts show more similar recurring language patterns within this corpus, and where does one focal text sit beside the others.

Compare groups
Group against group

Patterns across documented authors, genres, periods, or audiences, always measured relative to the corpus you assembled.

Trace style over time
Works in date order

How one writer's measured position varies across independently dated works. The dates order the corpus. Chronology alone does not establish ageing, maturation, a turning point, or a cause.
