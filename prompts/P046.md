## Result
- `NUM_TOPICS_DEFAULT` is 10. MALLET's `--num-topics` documentation gives 10 as the default ("a broad overview of the contents of the corpus") and scikit-learn's `LatentDirichletAllocation` has `n_components=10` (checked in the library's documentation; a test asserts it against the installed library). The slider's help text now says that no number is right for every corpus.
- `topic_stability()` and `stability_scores()` in `modelling.py`, and "Check topic stability (10 runs)" in the Overview tab. The measure is R_n of Agrawal, Fu and Menzies (2018): the model is fitted m times with different seeds; a topic of the first run is stable at size n in a run when some topic of that run shares at least n of its top 9 words; its score is the share of the m runs in which it is stable (the first run included, as in the paper's worked example), and R_n is the median score. m = 10 and at most 9 words, as in the paper (9 from the 7 ± 2 rule the authors cite). The app shows each topic's runs at n = 5, the size of the paper's worked example and first finding, and R_n for n = 1 to 9. Rieger, Jentsch and Rahnenführer (2024) use 100 runs by default; runs differ only in the seed, as in their replications, whereas Agrawal et al. also shuffle the document order. Seeds are the user's seed and the nine that follow it, fixed in advance.
- A test rebuilds the paper's worked example (topic scores 0.5, 0.75, 0.25 and 1 at n = 5, R_5 = 0.625) and gets the same numbers.
- Svevo trilogy (examples/, three documents, 7,923 terms, k = 10, seed 42, Mac with Apple silicon): one Gibbs model 1.7 s, the nine further runs 6.2 s. The three topics made of character names recur in 10 of 10 runs at n = 5; the other seven in 2 to 8.
- Agrawal et al.'s definitions were read in the arXiv version (v4); the published version was not compared.
## Follow-up (2 November 2026, afternoon)
On whether the coherence sweep should keep announcing an "optimal number of topics" from the highest C_v alone:

```
biz literatüre uygun davranmalıyız. ilan etmesin.
```

("We must act in line with the literature. It should not announce it.")

- The expander is now "Compare numbers of topics". It still plots C_v for 2 to 15 topics and states where C_v is highest, but names no optimal number and asks the user to run the analysis for the numbers to compare and read the topics. Sources in the text: Hoyle et al. (2021), whose automated evaluations declare winners that human evaluations do not; Chang et al. (2009), where models with better held-out likelihood can give less meaningful topics; Giménez Celano (2026), who notes there is little guidance on an optimal number of topics. Checked in the app with 20,000 words of *Una vita*.
- spaCy model size (decision 52, small models): spaCy's own evaluation (spacy-models, `meta/*-3.8.0.json`) gives lemma accuracy for small and large models of 97.15 and 97.53% (Italian), 90.76 and 91.36% (French), 97.50 and 97.94% (German), 96.31 and 96.60% (Spanish); English models report no lemma accuracy (tag accuracy 97.29 and 97.38%). These figures come from spaCy's evaluation on the corpora listed as the models' sources (for Italian, UD Italian ISDT 2.8, whose genres are legal, news and Wikipedia); the model record does not say which part of the corpus was used. They say nothing about literary or historical texts. No code change.
- Part-of-speech filter: already selectable (four presets including Custom); no code change.
- Vocabulary thresholds (min_df, max_df; decision 25) have no source. scikit-learn's CountVectorizer defaults to min_df = 1 and max_df = 1.0 and tomotopy to min_df = 0, so neither removes terms by document frequency. Published practice varies: Chang et al. (2009) remove terms in fewer than five documents; Hoyle et al. (2021) remove terms in more than 90% of documents and set the lower bound from corpus size. Under review; no code change yet.
