The method, in plain terms

What LDA does

Latent Dirichlet Allocation treats each document as a mixture of topics and each topic as a distribution over words. It surfaces sets of words that recur together; it does not read meaning, and it does not know which grouping matters for your question.

Choosing the number of topics

The number of topics is a parameter you set. There is no single correct value; different counts foreground different structure. Compare several, inspect the words, and report the setting you used, along with the random seed and preprocessing.

What stays visible

Lemmata shows a token-level preprocessing table (tokens kept, removed, lemmatized), reports coherence as a signal rather than a verdict, and fixes the random seed for reproducibility. Results describe the documented corpus under the chosen settings, not language in general.

Built for researchers who work with texts, not terminals.

Rigorous topic modeling for literary scholars, historians, and digital humanists, with the method kept in full view.

◧
No code required

Upload your corpus, choose your settings, and run LDA from your web browser with nothing to install. The analysis runs on the server that hosts the app. Rigorous analysis without writing a single line of code.

◔
Transparent preprocessing

A token-level table shows which tokens were kept or removed and which lemma each one received. Preprocessing stays visible.

✦
Five languages

Built-in support for English, Italian, French, German, and Spanish, with spaCy language models and per-language stopword lists. If your texts look more like another supported language than the one you chose, a warning appears above the results. Results have been checked against a published study for Italian only.

◈
Deterministic

With the same data, settings, random seed (42 by default), package and language-model versions, and processor type, every run gives identical results, so a result you cite is a result you can reproduce. Your settings, the main package versions and the processor type are recorded in an environment report, which the PDF report and the ZIP archive also contain.

▤
Export your results

CSV tables, a PDF report, a ZIP archive with the tables, metrics and environment report, and the heatmap as PNG or SVG.

◇
Coherence, in context

Coherence (C_v) is reported alongside the topics, framed as a signal to review qualitatively, never as a verdict.

◍
Gibbs sampling by default

Gibbs sampling, the method of MALLET, is the default, with MALLET's main default settings. The faster variational method, the only one before v0.3.0, can still be chosen. Other changes in v0.3.0 also affect results, so to reproduce an earlier result exactly, use the release that produced it. Earlier releases are archived on Zenodo.

◎
Stability and topic counts

"Check topic stability" fits the same model 9 more times with the seeds that follow yours and shows, for each topic, in how many of the 10 runs (yours included) a topic sharing at least 5 of its top 9 words appears (Agrawal, Fu and Menzies 2018). "Compare numbers of topics" plots coherence (C_v) for 2 to 15 topics and leaves the choice to you.

Three steps from corpus to insight.

Defaults get you started fast. You can change the analysis settings in the interface, and your choices are recorded in the environment report.

STEP 1
Upload

Drag and drop your text files (TXT, PDF, DOCX, ODT, EPUB) or paste text directly. With several long files, each file is split into parts of about the chunk size by default. Untick "Split every file into parts" to keep one document per file. Your data is processed in session and not stored permanently. To try it first, use the three Svevo novels in the examples folder of the source code (Italian, public domain).

STEP 2
Configure

Choose the language, number of topics, chunk size, part-of-speech filters, and stopwords. Sensible defaults are ready the moment you land.

STEP 3
Analyse

Explore the interactive topic map, topic charts and heatmap, together with topic distributions and word clouds. Download the tables and records as one ZIP archive, and the report as a PDF.
